# Asset sensitivity to shifts in Polymarket's expected Fed path

Generated by [`models/expectation_shocks.py`](../expectation_shocks.py).
Each Fed market's YES price is rebuilt at the **16:00 New York close** from
trades (median of the last 20 trades in the prior 24 h), for 7 meetings and
every day their markets traded: **269 trading days, Aug 2025 – Sep 2026**.

The daily shock is the change in the expected cumulative rate path (bp,
+ = hawkish), summed over meetings quoted on both days. It has a standard
deviation of 2.4 bp; the largest moves are 10–13 bp (14 days at 5 bp or
more). Asset returns are close-to-close, adjusted, so shock and return cover
the same window. Regressions use OLS with Newey-West errors (5 lags).

## Results

| Asset | Same-day β (% per bp) | t | Shock one day earlier (forecast): β | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPY | −0.015 | −0.70 | −0.060 | −2.40 |
| QQQ | −0.017 | −0.49 | −0.062 | −1.39 |
| IWM | −0.029 | −0.92 | −0.061 | −1.51 |
| DIA | −0.025 | −1.20 | −0.047 | −2.42 |
| TLT | −0.020 | −1.18 | −0.006 | −0.51 |

- **Same day:** no asset moves measurably with the shock. The signs are the
  expected ones (hawkish = down, TLT included), but none is significant.
- **Lead-lag hint:** a hawkish shift is followed by lower SPY and DIA the
  next day.
  - For: it survives dropping shocks over 10 bp (SPY t = −3.1) and has
    permutation p = 0.03.
  - Against: it is weak in the second half of the sample, it doesn't pass a
    Bonferroni correction across the 15 tests on the full sample, and the
    effect is small (about −0.14% per 1 SD shock). Treat it as a hypothesis
    to confirm on more meetings, not a result.
- **Wallets:** the hawkish flow of wallets that were skilled in earlier
  meetings does not lead the next day's shock (Spearman 0.08, n = 268). Its
  link to next-day returns is significant for one of five assets only,
  which is the chance rate.
- **Big-shock days:** dovish shifts of 5 bp or more coincided with every
  asset falling (−0.3% to −0.5%, TLT included, n = 14 days). That fits
  weak-growth news, not pure rate news.

## What would make this conclusive

- **More meetings:** the collector can fetch 2024–2025 Fed markets, which
  would roughly double the days and give an out-of-sample test of the
  lead-lag hint.
- **A rate-sensitive benchmark:** add a short-duration Treasury series (for
  example a 2-year yield or SHY) to validate that the shock measures what it
  should. TLT is long duration and only loosely tied to the next few
  meetings.
