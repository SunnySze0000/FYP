# Does wallet skill persist across Fed meetings?

Generated by [`models/wallet_persistence.py`](../wallet_persistence.py) from
every participant trade in 31 markets across **7 Fed meetings**
(Dec 2025 – Sep 2026; 3.85 million trade records), using only trades before
each market's decision cutoff. Outcomes: Dec 2025 cut, Jan–Jul 2026 hold
(5 meetings), Sep 2026 **hike**.

## Design

- Each wallet's score is its edge per share if trades were held to
  resolution, YES-equivalent: `d × (y − p)`.
- **Bias-adjusted score:** `d × (y − c(p))`, where `c(p)` is the
  price-to-outcome calibration curve fitted **only on other meetings**. Buying
  favourites systematically earns about zero on this score.
- **Walk-forward:** score wallets on all earlier meetings, test on the next
  meeting. Wallets need at least 10 trades in both periods (about 1,000 per
  test).
- **Controls:** a partial correlation removes formation-period style:
  hold-betting, hawkish-betting, price level, one-directional vs round-trip
  trading, and taker share. A one-directional subsample excludes
  market-maker-like wallets.

## Results (bias-adjusted score)

| Test meeting | ρ | Partial ρ (style controls) | ρ, one-directional wallets | Copy 24 h later, net of 0.5¢ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 2026 | 0.33 | 0.28 | 0.67 | +0.45¢ / share |
| Apr 2026 | 0.43 | 0.36 | 0.54 | +0.53¢ |
| Jun 2026 | 0.30 | 0.24 | 0.36 | +0.33¢ |
| Jul 2026 | 0.25 | 0.24 | 0.27 | −0.58¢ |
| Sep 2026 (hike) | −0.06 | 0.01 | −0.09 | −1.55¢ |

All per-meeting permutation p-values for Mar–Jul are below 0.01.

## Reading

1. **Skill persists across meetings,** not only within one. It isn't
   explained by favourite-buying (the bias-adjusted score), a hold or hawkish
   betting style, the price level paid, or market making. It's strongest
   among one-directional traders.
2. **It decays and then breaks.** ρ falls from Mar to Jul and is zero to
   negative at Sep 2026, the first hike after a run of holds. Skill learned
   in one regime didn't transfer to a regime change, which is exactly when it
   would have mattered most.
3. **It isn't a reliable trade.** Copying the formation-top-quintile
   wallets 24 h later keeps most of their edge in stable meetings
   (+0.8 to +1.0¢ before costs). But the Jul and Sep losses outweigh that:
   averaged over the 5 test meetings, copying loses about 0.16¢ per share at
   a 0.5¢ cost. Five test meetings are too few to call it either way
   (meeting-level sign test p = 0.38).

## Caveats

- **Few independent units.** Wallet-level p-values are small, but the
  regime-level evidence rests on 5 test meetings.
- **Coarse calibration.** `c(p)` is fitted from 31 markets, and the style
  controls are rank-linear.
- **Fills are approximate.** Costs are assumed (0–1¢ per share), not
  measured from order books, and copy fills use the last traded price.
