\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Background and Motivation}
Financial assets are repeatedly repriced around discrete events such as central-bank decisions, elections, and geopolitical developments. Investors can observe news, asset prices, and volatility indicators, but these sources do not directly show a continuously updated probability of the underlying event. Prediction markets such as Polymarket aggregate the beliefs of market participants into continuously updated probabilities, which can serve as a useful predictor of the real-world events \cite{wolfers2004}.

Beyond these aggregate probabilities, Polymarket adds a distinctive Web3 dimension through a hybrid trading architecture. Orders are created and matched through an off-chain central limit order book, while matched trades settle on Polygon. Its blockchain-based infrastructure provides transparent transaction histories that enable the behaviour and historical performance of individual participants to be analysed. This creates an opportunity to investigate whether information embedded in prediction-market activity can provide useful signals for traditional financial markets. 

This project aims to develop a machine-learning-assisted quantitative analysis framework that extracts information from Polymarket and evaluates its value in forecasting financial asset volatility. 

\subsubsection{Research question}

Could information embedded in Polymarket's on-chain trading activity improve conventional financial-market volatility forecasts and provide actionable insights for investors?

\subsection{Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Develop a smart-wallet identification model} using historical Polymarket on-chain trading behavior to distinguish informative traders from noise, hedging, and other non-predictive activity.
\item \textbf{Construct adjusted event probabilities} by weighting prediction-market information according to the estimated informativeness of smart wallets, and compare it with raw probability inferred from the bets.
\item \textbf{Develop a volatility forecasting model} that incorporates Polymarket-derived signals alongside a conventional market volatility baseline.
\item \textbf{Evaluate whether Polymarket data provides incremental predictive value} through historical backtesting and comparison against benchmark volatility forecasts, and present the results through an interactive AI-assisted dashboard.
\end{enumerate}

The primary target user is a research-oriented retail investor or finance professional who understands basic portfolio concepts but lacks the infrastructure to query blockchains, evaluate prediction-market participants, and construct event-conditioned risk models. EventLens is intended as a research and paper-decision tool rather than personalised financial advice.
